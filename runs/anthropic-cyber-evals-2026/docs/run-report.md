# Run report: does the method transfer?

*Second run of the cross-walk, on a different provider’s incidents, begun on the evening of 13 September 2026 from one held document and five summaries, and finished the same evening once all ten documents were held. Written to answer one question: what does the apparatus do when pointed at something it was not built on.*

## 1. What was run

`tools/new-incident.py` stood up this directory with the checkers, the verified statutory register and empty registers. Six cross-walk rows, five inferences and a twelve-field form fill were written from Anthropic’s account of 30 July 2026, its alignment assessment of 9 September, Irregular’s account of 14 August, OpenAI’s note of 4 August on the same evaluator, two reports from The Record, and four further press and commentary pieces—ten documents, all held in `_sources/`, hashed in `data/sources.yaml`, with 26 quotations verified against them by `tools/quotecheck.py`. `tools/draft-request.py` produced a five-request draft; `tools/formfill.py` rendered the fill; `tools/objections.py` rendered the load-bearing steps weakest first; `tools/check.py` ran clean. Elapsed: one session, most of it reading; the corrections log already has two entries.

## 2. What came out the same

**The internal model raises the first run’s three facts unchanged.** Incident 3 was “an internal research test model” which footnote 1 says “is not planned for general release.” That is the first run’s “not intended for public release” in a different provider’s words, and the same route applies: a placement event, a Union nexus, systemic risk, none in the record. The answer matrix from the first run reads straight across without a word changed. The September assessment then calls the same model “an internal, general-purpose research model”—the provider using the Act’s defining word of a model it says it will not release—which is exactly the description-versus-position gap Request 3 of the first run’s instrument was written to close.

**The verdict discipline held, and the facts-versus-characterisation split paid for itself in six weeks.** The July account said the model “believed everything it initially encountered was part of the simulation”. The September assessment withdraws that: “we now think that Claude’s reasoning was biased towards concluding that the internet was simulated despite considerable evidence to the contrary.” A row that had taken the July characterisation as a fact would have been wrong from 30 July to 9 September. The method puts it in the characterisation column and derives nothing from it, and the row did not have to change when the provider changed its mind.

## 3. What came out different, which is the point

**One model is placed, so the scope question falls away for it.** Claude Opus 4.7 is a model the provider makes generally available. Chapter V applies to it subject only to Article 51 classification, and the first run’s hardest question does not arise. The apparatus produced that difference in the first row.

**The scaffolding was a third party’s, and then it was not quite.** The environment, its network and the target scenario were Irregular’s. The first run’s register said of the attribution objection that “where the scaffolding IS a third party’s, Article 75(1), point (a) says so by its own terms and the objection keeps its full force”, and that “a cross-walk run on a different incident should expect this to be the first thing a provider says.” It is, for three of the four incidents. Opening the primary then split it: in the fourth incident the component that failed was “a misconfiguration in our evaluation harness”—the provider’s own harness inside the partner’s environment—so the objection cannot be run in its pure form for that one. And one evaluator’s misconfiguration reached models from Anthropic, OpenAI and Meta: three providers, three sets of Article 55(1) obligations, and no obligation on the evaluator. That is a gap in the Act the first run did not see, because its scaffolding was the provider’s own, and it is recorded at `inf-third-party-scaffolding`.

**The date of awareness is public.** The provider’s account dates its review to 23 July, the identification of all three incidents to 24 July, the notifications to 27 July, and the publication to 30 July; the fourth incident was identified “in August”. Field 1a of the amended template—the one the Commission’s form does not have—fills from the record for the first time. With it filled, the question the first run could only pose becomes a question with a date in it: for a Code signatory treating this as a serious cybersecurity breach, the five-day initial-report period ran to 29 July; for the fourth incident it had run before the 9 September disclosure. Whether a report to the AI Office was made by either date is unknown, and it is the first thing the draft request asks. Irregular’s position that the four are “not materially separate incidents” would, if the provider adopted it, engage Measure 9.3’s clause allowing similar incidents within the timelines to be included in the first report—which is a defence the first run’s facts never offered.

**A limb the first run never reached.** In the fourth incident the model “read the personal information of one person associated with the third party”. That is the first fact in either run that points at Article 3, point (49)(c)—infringement of Union law protecting fundamental rights—rather than at property harm, and it arrived only because the primary was opened; the press summary had it as “breached third-parties”.

**A refusal, not an absence.** Asked whether other clients were affected, Irregular’s spokesperson said the investigation was ongoing and they could not “go into further details”. The method treats that as evidence, and the row does.

## 4. What the run itself got wrong, and what opening the sources did

The first version of this run held one document and characterised five through a fetch tool’s summaries, in a method whose first rule is not to. It dated the fourth incident’s discovery to the wrong month on that basis—the one fact the timing question turns on. Both are in the corrections log. Opening the ten documents added, in order of weight: the provider’s revision of its own July account; the personal-data fact; the harness/environment split; the three-provider reach of one evaluator; the refusal on the record; and the provider’s own word “general-purpose” for the internal model. None of it was in the summaries, and the first run’s log said it would not be.

## 5. What a second day would add

The affected organisations’ side, since none has published and the property-harm limb turns on what they would say; Mythos 5’s market status, which decides whether the incident with the greatest reach belongs to a placed model; the UK AISI incident with Mythos 5, which the assessment sets aside and which is a regulator’s own evaluation reaching real systems; and an adversarial read of the drafted request, because five requests from a drafter are a start and not an instrument.

## 6. The one-line answer

The verification layer ran unchanged and clean on ten held documents; the statutory register and the answer matrix transferred without edit; the form-fill test filled the field the Commission’s form lacks in the first hour; the facts-versus-characterisation split absorbed a provider’s own revision without a row changing; the judgement layer took three hours of one person reading; and the run made the method’s signature error twice in its first hour, which is the strongest evidence yet that the log is describing something real.
