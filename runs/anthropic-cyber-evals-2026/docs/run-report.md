# Run report: does the method transfer?

*Second run of the cross-walk, on a different provider’s incidents, made in one evening on 13 September 2026 from one held document and five unheld ones. Written to answer one question: what does the apparatus do when pointed at something it was not built on.*

## 1. What was run

`tools/new-incident.py` stood up this directory with the checkers, the verified statutory register and empty registers. Six cross-walk rows, five inferences and a twelve-field form fill were written from Anthropic’s account of 30 July 2026 (held, hashed, quotations verified) and from fetch summaries of four other documents (registered by URL, quotations unverified). `tools/draft-request.py` produced a five-request draft; `tools/formfill.py` rendered the fill; `tools/objections.py` rendered the load-bearing steps weakest first; `tools/check.py` ran clean. Elapsed: under two hours, most of it reading.

## 2. What came out the same

**The internal model raises the first run’s three facts unchanged.** Incident 3 was “an internal research test model” which footnote 1 says “is not planned for general release.” That is the first run’s “not intended for public release” in a different provider’s words, and the same route applies: a placement event, a Union nexus, systemic risk, none in the record. The answer matrix from the first run reads straight across without a word changed. That is the strongest evidence the evening produced that the scope analysis is about the Act and not about one incident.

**The verdict discipline held without adjustment.** Every row took one of the three permitted openers; the facts-versus-characterisation split had somewhere to put “basic techniques”, “three isolated incidents” and “did not deliberately attempt to escape”, which are the provider’s framings and would otherwise have leaked into verdicts.

## 3. What came out different, which is the point

**One model is placed, so the scope question falls away for it.** Claude Opus 4.7 is a model the provider makes generally available. Chapter V applies to it subject only to Article 51 classification, and the first run’s hardest question does not arise. The apparatus produced that difference in the first row.

**The scaffolding was a third party’s.** The environment, its network and the target scenario were Irregular’s. The first run’s register said, of the attribution objection, that “where the scaffolding IS a third party’s, Article 75(1), point (a) says so by its own terms and the objection keeps its full force”, and that “a cross-walk run on a different incident should expect this to be the first thing a provider says.” It is. The register predicted the shape of the next incident’s strongest objection before the incident was chosen, which is the kind of thing a method should be able to do and rarely can. What blunts it is recorded at `inf-third-party-scaffolding`, and it blunts less here than there.

**The date of awareness is public.** The provider’s account dates its review to 23 July, the identification of all three incidents to 24 July, the notifications to 27 July, and the publication to 30 July. Field 1a of the amended template—the one the Commission’s form does not have—fills from the record for the first time. With it filled, the question the first run could only pose becomes a question with a date in it: for a Code signatory treating this as a serious cybersecurity breach, the five-day initial-report period ran to 29 July; whether a report to the AI Office was made by then is unknown, and it is the first thing the draft request asks. The instrumentation finding stands on its feet on the second incident as it did on the first: the form still cannot show whether that report was timely, and here the analyst could.

**The fourth incident is a timing question of its own.** Reported to have occurred in January, been discovered in July and been disclosed on 10 September, it puts roughly two months between awareness and public disclosure. A report to the AI Office may sit anywhere in that gap; nothing public says.

## 4. What the evening did not do, and a second day would

Hold the sources. One of six is held; Irregular’s own account, Anthropic’s September update on the fourth incident, the CSA note and the press reports are registered by URL and quoted unverified. The first run’s log has fourteen entries for exactly that state, and this run should expect the same. Ask the affected organisations’ side: none has published, and the property-harm limb turns on what they would say. Register Mythos 5’s market status, which decides whether Incident 2, the most serious in reach, belongs to a placed model or not. And run the request past the same adversarial read the first run had, because the five requests the drafter produced are a start and not an instrument.

## 5. The one-line answer

The verification layer ran unchanged and clean; the statutory register and the answer matrix transferred without edit; the form-fill test produced a new finding in the first hour; and the judgement layer took two hours of one person reading, as the first run said it would. A different incident produced different verdicts from the same rows, and one prediction the first run made about the next incident came true.
