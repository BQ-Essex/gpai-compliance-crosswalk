A provider’s counsel will reach for these in roughly this order. Each row says where the step is stated so the answer can be found; the register itself, and `python3 tools/infercheck.py --attack`, are the source. Not a document of any Union body.*

A **step** inference flips wholly on one fact; a **graded** one moves the conclusion rather than breaking it. Strength is the author’s own estimate and is the softest thing here.

| 4 | `inf-scaffolding-attribution`—The conduct in the incident is attributable to the general-purpose AI MODEL and so to its provider, rather than to downstream scaffolding - the harness, memory, execution loop and tool access - which is where agentic behaviour largely resides and which may be another actor’s. | contestable / graded | THE OBJECTION THAT OUTLIVES A SCOPE FINDING, though not the provider’s strongest - scope and territory defeat everything, where this defeats attribution under Article 55(1), points (c) and (d) alone and leaves Article 52(1) and Article 55(1), point (b) untouched. One reply the earlier version of this entry missed: Article 3, point (49), Measure 9.3 of the Code and Guidelines para. Persistent goal-seeking, memory and tool invocation are properties of scaffolding rather than of raw weights; if the scaffolding was built by a different actor, the conduct may not be the model provider’s at all. What blunts it here, and only partly, is that the provider’s own account describes the evaluation environment as its own and names the absent safeguards as its own - “system prompts, harnesses, and control mechanisms” - so on these facts the scaffolding appears to be the same undertaking’s. ARTICLE 75(1), POINT (a) BLUNTS IT FURTHER, AND NOT COMPLETELY. The AI Office is exclusively competent for the supervision and enforcement of AI systems “based on general-purpose AI models where the model and the system are developed by the same provider, or by providers forming part of the same undertaking as that provider”, and Art. 75(1a) routes those systems’ serious-incident reports to the AI Office with Art. 73(2) to (9) applying mutatis mutandis. So the Act already contemplates model plus own scaffolding as a regulated unit under one authority, and the “same undertaking” limb defeats the corporate-structure version of the objection, where a subsidiary built the harness. BE PRECISE ABOUT WHAT THAT DOES: it is a JURISDICTIONAL answer, not an ATTRIBUTION one. It shows the objection opens no regulatory gap - the provider cannot say “that was the system, not the model” and thereby leave the AI Office’s competence. It does not show that the scaffolded system’s conduct is the model’s for Art. 55 purposes. And where the scaffolding IS a third party’s, Art. 75(1), point (a) says so by its own terms and the objection keeps its full force. That is a fact about this incident, not a general answer, and a cross-walk run on a different incident should expect this to be the first thing a provider says. | docs/report-draft.md §5.1 |

The second limb is what a challenge would be argued from, AND IT BITES UNEVENLY. Recital 97 has two limbs: an own system MADE AVAILABLE ON THE MARKET, where the embedded model is supplied for use on the Union market in the course of a commercial activity and Article 3, points (9) and (10) are arguably just being applied; and an own system merely PUT INTO SERVICE, where nothing is supplied to anyone and putting into service is the trigger Article 2(1), point (a) withholds from models. **How to read it.** The first rows are where the analysis expects to be attacked and where a reply should be prepared before the response to the request is opened. Nothing above is a prediction of what any provider will in fact say.
